# Movie Review: Arrival (2016)

**Director:** Denis Villeneuve · **Starring:** Amy Adams, Jeremy Renner, Forest Whitaker
**Watched:** October 10, 2026, at home with roommates
**My rating:** 4.5 / 5

## Summary

Twelve alien spacecraft land around the world, and the U.S. Army recruits linguist Louise Banks to figure out how to communicate with the visitors. As she slowly decodes their circular written language, her sense of time begins to change, and the film reframes everything you thought you were watching.

## What worked

- **The pacing.** It is a slow film, but every scene earns its place. The first trip into the ship is tense without any action at all.
- **Amy Adams.** Her performance carries the movie. She makes the work of learning a language feel exciting.
- **The score and sound design.** Jóhann Jóhannsson's music sounds almost alien itself, and the low hums inside the ship are unsettling.
- **The twist.** The reveal about Louise's memories is handled quietly, and it made me want to rewatch the opening immediately.

## What did not work as well

- The subplot with the other nations turning hostile felt rushed and a little convenient.
- Jeremy Renner's character does not get much to do besides support Louise.

## Favorite moment

The scene where Louise takes off her hazmat suit to show the heptapods her face. It is a simple choice that says a lot about trust and communication.

## Takeaways

The film is really about how language shapes the way we think, which ties in nicely with what we covered in my linguistics elective this semester. It also asks a hard question: would you choose a life if you knew exactly how it would end?

## Would I recommend it?

Yes, especially to anyone who likes thoughtful science fiction over action-heavy blockbusters. Watch it with no spoilers and with your full attention.
